Binge.

Privacy Policy — Last updated September 9, 2026

Binge is an Android app for browsing, discovering, and tracking movies and TV shows. This policy explains what information is collected when you use Binge, how it is used, and your choices regarding that information.

Information we collect

What you do in the app

Your search queries, ratings, watchlist additions, favorites, and any custom lists you create are sent to The Movie Database (TMDB) so they can be saved to your TMDB account and synced across devices. Binge does not operate its own backend server — all account data lives with TMDB.

All local data is stored in Android DataStore and a Room database on your device. Uninstalling the app removes all of it, with one exception — the sign-in backup described below. Images you chose to save to your Pictures folder are yours and stay where you put them.

Restoring sign-in on a new device Google Block Store

So you don't have to sign in again after switching phones, your TMDB sign-in can be restored on a new device. Only the TMDB session token and account ID are backed up — through Google Block Store, part of Google Play services — so Binge can sign you back into your own TMDB account when you set up a new device or restore from a backup.

Your password is never involved — Binge never sees it. Google encrypts the backup, and the session token is one you can revoke at any time from your TMDB settings. Signing out of Binge deletes this backup.

Crash reports on by default Firebase Crashlytics

When the app crashes — and when it hits an error it recovers from, such as a failed network request — a report is sent to Google Firebase Crashlytics so the bug can be found and fixed. It contains the stack trace, your device model, Android version and app version, plus recent diagnostic log entries — which can include the name of the screen you were on and the address of a request that failed.

Reports are not linked to your TMDB account. Like usage analytics they carry a randomly generated identifier for your app installation, so repeated crashes on one device can be recognised as the same device — making them pseudonymous rather than anonymous. Unlike analytics, crash reporting is on by default; you can turn it off in Account → Preferences → Send crash reports.

Usage analytics opt-in — off by default Firebase Analytics PostHog

Analytics is off until you turn it on. During setup Binge asks whether you want to share usage data (screens viewed, features used). Nothing is collected unless you actively agree — declining, or closing the app without choosing, leaves it off.

If you do agree, the same events are sent to two analytics providers. PostHog processes them on servers in the European Union. Google Firebase Analytics is operated by Google, which processes data on its own infrastructure outside the EEA under Google's privacy policy and terms.

These events record that something happened, not what it was. For example, a search is recorded as "a search was performed" — your search terms are never included. The point is aggregate: we log how the app is used — which features and actions people reach for — but no event tells us who you are or the specific titles you were looking at.

They are not fully anonymous, and we would rather say so than overstate it. Each provider attaches a randomly generated identifier for your app installation, and it is that identifier — not your IP address — that links events from the same device together. Your device's IP address does reach each provider with the request, and is used to infer your approximate location, which is stored alongside each event — typically your country and region, and in some cases down to a city or approximate coordinates. PostHog is configured to discard the IP address itself once that location has been derived. This is an estimate derived from the IP address, not your device's GPS: Binge never requests location permission and never sends your precise location. That identifier is not linked to your TMDB account and is discarded when you uninstall the app, but it does mean the data is pseudonymous rather than anonymous.

You can disable analytics at any time in Account → Preferences → Share analytics data. Turning it off stops all future collection by both providers. Previously collected data is governed by Google's privacy policy and PostHog's privacy policy.

Third-party services

Binge relies on the following third-party services. Each has its own privacy policy.

  • The Movie Database (TMDB) — provides all content and stores your account data. TMDB Privacy Policy
  • Google — Firebase (Analytics, Crashlytics, Remote Config) for analytics, crash reporting and remote feature configuration, and Google Play services (Block Store) to back up your sign-in for restore on a new device. Google Privacy Policy
  • Gravatar (Automattic) — serves your TMDB profile picture when you have one. Loading it sends your device's IP address to Gravatar. Automattic Privacy Policy
  • PostHog — product analytics, hosted in the European Union. Receives the same usage events as Firebase Analytics, and only while analytics is enabled. PostHog Privacy Policy

Permissions

Binge requests only the permissions it needs:

  • Internet — required to load content from TMDB and communicate with the analytics and crash-reporting services listed above.
  • Write external storage — required on Android 9 and below to save a poster or backdrop into your device's Pictures folder, when you choose to save one. Binge's own image cache is private to the app and doesn't use it.
  • Notifications — optional, and only requested if you connect a media-request server and turn its notifications on. Binge never sends marketing notifications.

Binge does not request access to your camera, microphone, location, contacts, or any other sensitive data.

Account sign-in

Binge does not have its own account system. Signing in means authorising Binge to access your existing TMDB account via TMDB's OAuth flow. Your TMDB credentials are entered directly on the TMDB website — Binge never sees your password. The resulting session token is stored locally on your device and sent only to the TMDB API. It is also backed up through Google Block Store so your sign-in can be restored on a new device, as described under “Restoring sign-in on a new device” above. You can revoke access at any time from your TMDB connected applications settings, or by signing out inside the app.

Connecting a media-request server is separate, and works differently: that server has its own accounts, so you may enter its username or email and password directly in Binge. Those are sent only to the server address you supplied — never to us, and never to any third party — and only the resulting access key or login session is kept on your device, encrypted.

Data retention and deletion

  • Uninstalling the app removes all local data, apart from any images you deliberately saved to your Pictures folder. The cross-device sign-in backup is held by Google Play services rather than the app, so to remove it, sign out first (or delete your device backup from your Google account).
  • Signing out removes your TMDB session, the cross-device sign-in backup, your recent searches, and the cached copies of your watchlist, favourites, ratings and lists. It does not clear settings kept on the device, such as your chosen streaming services, default tab, and any connected server details — those stay until you change them or uninstall the app.
  • TMDB account data (watchlists, ratings, lists) can be managed and deleted directly from your TMDB account settings.
  • Analytics and crash data is kept only as long as it is useful for the purpose it was collected for:
    • PostHog — events are retained for 12 months, then deleted.
    • Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics — retained for the periods set in our Google Analytics and Firebase settings and by Google's own retention policies. Ask us and we will tell you the current values.
    Disabling analytics in the app stops further collection by both providers. It does not delete what was collected before — you can ask us to, under “Your rights”.

Your rights

You have the rights below over your personal data. Binge is unusual in holding almost none of it itself — your account data lives with TMDB, and your usage and crash data with the analytics providers — so each right names the fastest route. We will help with any of them if you email us.

  • Access — a copy of the data held about you. TMDB account data: from your TMDB account. Analytics and crash data: ask us and we will request it from the provider for your app installation.
  • Rectification — correcting data that is wrong. Your ratings, watchlist and lists are editable in the app or on TMDB.
  • Erasure — deletion. Uninstalling removes everything held on the device; signing out first also deletes the cross-device sign-in backup. TMDB account data is deleted from your TMDB account settings. For analytics and crash data, email us and we will have the provider delete the records for your app installation.
  • Restriction and objection — you can stop any collection that is not needed to run the app, at any time, in Account → Preferences: Share analytics data and Send crash reports.
  • Portability — your watchlist, ratings and lists are held by TMDB and exportable from your TMDB account. Binge stores nothing about you that it did not get from TMDB.
  • Withdrawing consent — analytics runs on your consent, and you can withdraw it at any time in Account → Preferences → Share analytics data. Withdrawing stops all future collection immediately. It does not make the collection before that point unlawful.

We will answer any of these within one month, free of charge.
